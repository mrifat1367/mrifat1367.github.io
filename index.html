I study how the mutational process itself evolves.

Rate evolution · C. elegans

Do different kinds of mutation evolve differently?

Using long-term mutation-accumulation lines of C. elegans, we asked whether base-substitution and indel mutation rates evolve under the same rules. The answer is no: SNV rates behave as the cost-of-fidelity hypothesis predicts, while indel rates are consistent with drift-barrier dynamics — two mutation classes, two evolutionary regimes.

Published · Genetics (2026) · co-first author

Comparative MA · C. briggsae

Mutation rates and spectra across genetic backgrounds

A mutation-accumulation experiment in two wild genetic backgrounds of C. briggsae (HK104 and PB800), sequenced with both Illumina and PacBio HiFi. I built the full pipeline — from variant calling and false-negative-calibrated rate estimation to trinucleotide-context spectra, indels, and structural variants — to ask how the mutational process differs between backgrounds and from its sister species.

Manuscript in preparation

Population genomics · three species

Mutational signatures in the wild

Do the spectra we measure in the lab match what natural populations actually carry? Using wild-isolate genomes of C. elegans, C. briggsae, and C. tropicalis from CaeNDR, I compare polymorphism spectra to mutation spectra and test the forces that bend them — including GC-biased gene conversion along the recombination landscape.

Manuscript in preparation

Methods

Getting long-read mutation calling right

Long reads promise a complete view of spontaneous mutation — including the structural variants short reads miss — but they come with their own artifacts. I'm writing a field guide to the bioinformatic pitfalls of long-read mutation-accumulation studies, from caller disagreement to alignment-induced ghosts.
